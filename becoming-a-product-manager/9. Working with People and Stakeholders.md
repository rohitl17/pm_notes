# Working with People and Stakeholders

As a Product Manager, your success often depends less on coding or design skills and more on how effectively you **communicate, collaborate, and align** with the people around you.  
This chapter focuses on how to work with key stakeholders — engineers, designers, executives, and others — while maintaining trust, clarity, and momentum.

---

## 1. General Communication Skills

**Goal:**  
Ensure everyone on your team understands the vision, goals, and context behind every product decision.

**Core Principles:**
- **Clarity over jargon:**  
  Translate complex ideas into simple, actionable language.  
  Example: Instead of saying *“We need to reduce technical debt,”* say *“We need to fix the slow loading time that frustrates users.”*

- **Consistency:**  
  Communicate updates regularly through standups, sprint reviews, and async channels like Slack or Confluence.

- **Empathy and listening:**  
  Great PMs listen more than they speak — they actively understand pain points of engineers, designers, and users.

- **Storytelling:**  
  Frame product updates or roadmaps as stories that connect the “why” to the “what.”  
  Example: *“Last quarter, customers struggled with onboarding — our goal this sprint is to cut that setup time in half.”*

**PM Mindset:**  
Communication is not just about sharing information — it’s about building alignment and trust across diverse personalities and goals.

---

## 2. Working with Engineers

**Goal:**  
Build a relationship of **mutual respect** where engineers feel ownership over the product and not just execution partners.

**Key Practices:**
- **Be technically conversant:**  
  Understand the basics of APIs, databases, or architecture — not to code, but to empathize and discuss trade-offs intelligently.

- **Clarity in specs:**  
  Clearly define *what* needs to be built and *why*. Leave the *how* to engineering.  
  Example: “We want to reduce user churn by improving load times,” not “Use React for this feature.”

- **Respect their time:**  
  Engineers value uninterrupted focus. Avoid unnecessary meetings and prioritize well-defined tickets.

- **Be a problem-solver:**  
  When bugs or blockers arise, collaborate to remove obstacles.

**Example:**  
A PM notices users dropping off during checkout. Instead of demanding “Fix this bug ASAP,” they ask, “What’s causing latency here, and what options do we have to improve response time?”

**Pro Tip:**  
Engineers respond best when they see the **impact** of their work on users — bring real feedback and metrics to discussions.

---

## 3. Working with Designers

**Goal:**  
Create synergy between user experience and business outcomes — ensuring every product decision serves both user delight and company goals.

**Best Practices:**
- **Start early:**  
  Involve designers from the discovery phase, not just before development starts.
- **Co-create, don’t dictate:**  
  Bring the problem, not the solution.  
  Example: “Users abandon onboarding midway” → Let designers explore interaction fixes.
- **Respect the design process:**  
  Give space for iteration and user feedback cycles — avoid rushing mockups into development.
- **Balance aesthetics with usability:**  
  Use data and feedback to decide when design perfection gives diminishing returns.

**Example:**  
A designer proposes a new dashboard layout. Instead of debating colors, the PM asks, “How does this help users find insights faster?”

**Pro Tip:**  
Celebrate design wins with data — show how visual improvements boosted conversions or engagement.

---

## 4. Working with Executives and Others

**Goal:**  
Translate between strategy and execution — helping executives see how day-to-day work aligns with big-picture company goals.

**Key Strategies:**
- **Speak their language:**  
  Focus on *outcomes*, not *outputs*.  
  Example: “This feature can increase retention by 8%” instead of “We released version 2.0 of the app.”
- **Be concise and data-driven:**  
  Execs want clear summaries — metrics, progress, blockers, and next steps.
- **Anticipate questions:**  
  Think ahead about ROI, risks, and dependencies. Be prepared with trade-off analysis.
- **Manage expectations:**  
  Be honest about timelines and feasibility. Underpromise and overdeliver where possible.

**Example:**  
When presenting to leadership, a PM might use a one-pager outlining:
- Goal: Increase self-service revenue
- Metric: +15% upsell conversion
- Timeline: 2 months
- Risk: Limited dev capacity
- Next Step: MVP test with pricing options

**Pro Tip:**  
Executives appreciate PMs who *own outcomes* — not just coordinate tasks.

---

### Summary

| Stakeholder | PM Focus | Key Skill |
|--------------|-----------|-----------|
| **Engineers** | Empower and clarify problems | Technical empathy |
| **Designers** | Co-create user experiences | Balancing usability & business goals |
| **Executives** | Communicate vision and progress | Strategic storytelling |
| **Everyone** | Build trust through clarity | Empathy and consistent communication |

**Final Thought:**  
A Product Manager is more of a bridge and less of a boss.  Your influence comes from clarity, empathy, and your ability to align diverse people toward a common product goal.
