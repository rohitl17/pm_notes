# Product Vision

## 1. What Is a Product Vision?
A **product vision** is a clear, inspiring statement that answers the fundamental question:

> **“Why are we building this product?”**

It provides long-term direction, guides decision-making, and aligns teams toward a common goal. A strong product vision does not describe features or short-term goals — it describes the future you want to create.

---

## 2. Examples of Powerful Product Visions

### **SpaceX**
> *“Make human life multi-planetary.”*  
A single, bold, long-term aspiration guiding every product and engineering decision.

### **Antoine de Saint-Exupéry Quote**
> *“If you want to build a ship, don’t drum up people to collect wood and assign tasks. Instead, teach them to long for the vast and endless sea.”*

This perfectly captures the lesson:  
**Vision inspires. Instructions do not.**

## 3. The Product Vision Playbook

The course breaks product vision into six key components. These components help teams craft a meaningful, actionable, and inspiring vision.

---

### ### 3.1 Define Your Product Opportunity Space
Clarify **where** the opportunity lies.

Questions to consider:
- What market problem or gap are we targeting?
- What customer segment experiences this the most?
- What macro trends (AI, regulation, competition) drive urgency?
- Why is *now* the right time?

The opportunity space sets the boundaries for your long-term direction.

---

### 3.2 Establish Product Principles
Product principles define **what the product stands for** and **what it will never try to be**.

Examples of product principles:
- “AI should augment human decision-making, not replace it.”
- “Speed and usability above feature complexity.”
- “Privacy-first for all user data.”

Good principles provide guardrails that simplify decision-making and keep the team aligned.

---

### 3.3 Build Personas and Empathy Maps
Personas help articulate the **user** and **customer**.  
Empathy maps help define:
- What they think  
- What they feel  
- What they see  
- What they hear  
- What they struggle with  
- What motivates them  

This ensures the vision is anchored in real human needs, not internal assumptions.

---

### 3.4 Craft Your Vision Statement
Your vision statement should be:
- **Concise** (one sentence)
- **Aspirational**
- **Future-focused**
- **Independent of specific features**

**Example format:**
> *“Empower [target user] to achieve [desired outcome] through [core idea].”*

This single statement becomes the north star for strategy, roadmap, and execution.

---

### 3.5 Develop Strong Communication Habits
A great vision must be **communicated frequently and consistently**.

Best practices:
- Repeat the vision across rituals (kickoffs, planning, retros, onboarding).
- Use storytelling instead of jargon.
- Share examples of how teams are living the vision.
- Make the vision visible in documents, dashboards, and design systems.

Communication turns a vision into a culture, not just a sentence.

---

### 3.6 Link Vision to Success Metrics
A vision is aspirational — but it must still be measurable.

Ask:
- How will we know we’re moving toward this vision?
- What metrics validate long-term success?
- What signals indicate customer value creation?

Success metrics may include:
- Adoption  
- Activation  
- Retention  
- NPS  
- Productivity improvements  
- Cost savings  
- Revenue growth  

Metrics keep the vision grounded in reality.

---

## 4. Why Product Vision Matters (especially for Data Science and AI)
A strong product vision helps Data Scientists:

- Avoid building models that don’t align with long-term goals  
- Focus on solving meaningful customer problems  
- Prioritize which ML features matter  
- Communicate DS impact in terms leadership understands  
- Connect technical outcomes to business outcomes  
- Guide experimentation and iteration toward the right future  

Vision is the foundation that ensures DS/ML efforts lead to real, sustained product impact.
