# Exploring the Opportunity Space

Before building a product, it is crucial to define the **opportunity space**—the set of problems, segments, and possibilities that the product could pursue. The opportunity space helps teams understand:

- What they can do  
- What they should do  
- What they will not do  

A meaningful opportunity space is built from three layers:

1. **Internal Context Analysis**  
2. **External Context Analysis**  
3. **Problem–Solution Matching**

---

## 1. Internal Context Analysis

Internal context analysis focuses on understanding the strengths, capabilities, and constraints within your own organization or team.

Key questions:
- **Who are we?**  
  (Identity, mission, capabilities)

- **What do we know?**  
  (Domain expertise, technologies, datasets)

- **Who do we know?**  
  (Customers, partners, distribution channels)

- **What are we good at?**  
  (Technical strengths, e.g., CV, NLP, time-series, ML engineering)

- **What will we *not* pursue?**  
  (Deliberate scoping boundaries)

### Examples:
- A Data Scientist skilled in machine learning might focus on:
  - Computer vision models  
  - Time-series forecasting  
  - Generative AI systems  
  - SaaS analytics  

- At Fortive, the opportunity space is shaped by:
  - Manufacturing SaaS  
  - Industrial IoT  
  - Operations, service, safety, energy management  
  → Not retail or entertainment domains.

Internal context helps clarify **where you can win** and **where you will not play**.

---

## 2. External Context Analysis

External context analysis examines the **market**, **industry**, and **environmental trends**.

Key questions:
- What opportunities does the market offer?  
- What constraints does the external environment impose?  
- What unmet needs exist?  
- What emerging technologies or solutions are becoming viable?  
- What trends are reshaping customer expectations?  
- What are competitors doing?  

### Example: Generative AI  
When GenAI emerged, it unlocked new possibilities:  
- Question-answering systems  
- Proposal generation  
- Knowledge assistants  
- Semantic search  
- Code generation  
- Automated document workflows  

Understanding these trends shapes the opportunity space and helps teams anticipate where the market is headed.

### External factors include:
- New technologies  
- Regulatory changes  
- Market shifts  
- Competitive landscape  
- Customer adoption readiness  

External analysis ensures the opportunity space is not only possible, but **timely**.

---

## 3. Problem–Solution Matching

Once internal and external contexts are understood, the next step is to match:
- **The problems that exist in the world**  
- **With the solutions you can deliver**

This is where value creation becomes concrete.

Key questions:
- What **value** do we aspire to create?  
- For **which customers** or segments?  
- What **problems** have we validated?  
- Do our capabilities allow us to deliver a compelling **solution**?  
- Is this solution better than alternatives?  

### Example:
If proposal writers struggle with repetitive RFP drafting and you already have:
- A GenAI model  
- Domain-specific embeddings  
- Context indexing capability  
→ You can match their problem to your solution.

Problem–solution matching ensures the opportunity space is grounded in **real needs** and **real ability to execute**.

---

## 4. Why Opportunity Space Matters (Especially in DS/ML)

For Data Scientists and ML Engineers, defining opportunity space helps:

- Avoid building models no one needs  
- Align projects with business impact  
- Prioritize feasible, high-value use cases  
- Evaluate whether ML is even the right tool  
- Choose domains where data and expertise exist  
- Focus effort where differentiation is possible  
- Ensure model development aligns with internal strengths and external trends  

Opportunity space helps DS/ML teams build **strategic**, not accidental, solutions.
