# Product Market Fit — Notes from *Introduction to Product Strategy*

## 1. What Is Product-Market Fit (PMF)?
Product-Market Fit is the alignment between what a product offers and what a specific market needs. A product achieves PMF when:

- It solves a real, meaningful problem  
- For a clearly defined target customer  
- In a way that customers prefer over existing alternatives  
- Leading to strong adoption, usage, and willingness to pay  

---

## 2. Where PMF Sits in Product Frameworks
In most product frameworks, PMF sits at the intersection of:

- **Value Proposition** — what the product promises to deliver  
- **Underserved Customer Needs** — gaps in existing solutions or unmet pain points  

PMF is achieved when the product’s value proposition closes the gap created by customer needs.

UX → Features → Value Proposition ←→ Underserved Needs (PMF lives here)


---

## 3. How to Achieve Product-Market Fit

### The Design Thinking Iterative Loop
Achieving PMF is not a one-time activity; it is an iterative process driven by user insights.

1. **Empathize**  
   Understand user motivations, pains, and behaviors.

2. **Define**  
   Identify the core user problem worth solving.

3. **Ideate**  
   Create multiple solution concepts.

4. **Prototype**  
   Build quick, testable versions of the solution.

5. **Test**  
   Validate with real users; refine based on feedback.

This loop repeats until the solution meets user needs effectively.

---

## 4. Product Excellence Framework
Product excellence sits at the intersection of:

- **Strategy** — clear choices about markets, customers, and goals.  
- **Design** — intuitive and delightful user experiences.  
- **Execution** — consistent, reliable delivery of high-quality solutions.

Teams that excel across all three dimensions deliver outstanding products.

---

## 5. The STAT Document
A STAT document brings alignment and clarity before building a product. It answers:

- **What** are we building?  
- **Why** are we building it?  
- **Who** are we building it for?  

This ensures that every product decision is rooted in purpose and solves a validated problem.

---

## 6. Product Strategy and Business Strategy
Product strategy must always work **backwards** from business strategy.

### Typical Business Strategy Goals
- Increase revenue  
- Reduce operational cost  
- Expand into new markets  
- Improve customer retention or satisfaction  

### Therefore, Product Strategy Should:
- Tie directly to a business-level objective  
- Justify how the product creates measurable impact  
- Ensure the “why” behind the product aligns with company priorities  

A product can only succeed long-term when it contributes meaningfully to the business.
