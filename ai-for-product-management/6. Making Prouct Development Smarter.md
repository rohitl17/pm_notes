# Module 6 — The Product Management Lifecycle (PMLC)

The **Product Management Lifecycle** is a cyclical framework explaining how great products are conceived, validated, built, launched, and continuously improved.  
The PMLC emphasizes that **business outcomes matter more than features**.

There are **7 phases**:

0. Define Business Outcomes  
1. Discover  
2. Validate  
3. Build  
4. Launch  
5. Evaluate  
6. Iterate  

---

# Phase 0 — Define Business Outcomes

Before anything else, PMs must answer:

- **What business outcome do we want to achieve?**  
- **Why does this product or feature matter now?**  
- **How does it connect to company strategy or revenue/cost goals?**  

Outcomes guide decision-making in every subsequent phase.

Examples:
- Reduce onboarding time by 40%  
- Increase activation by 15%  
- Improve retention of key accounts  
- Lower support ticket volume  

---

# Phase 1 — Discover

The goal of the Discover phase is to uncover:

- Customer pain points  
- Market gaps  
- Opportunities that align with business outcomes  

Key activities:
- Customer interviews  
- Shadowing sessions  
- Studying usage analytics  
- Identifying blockers to adoption  

Discovery must avoid leading questions. PMs should ask:
- “Tell me about the last time you…”
- “What is the hardest part about…?”
- “Why is this important to you?”

### Using AI in Discovery
AI helps PMs:
- Analyze large amounts of qualitative data (NPS, ticket logs, interviews)  
- Distinguish signals from noise  
- Compare patterns between **retained users vs churned users**  
- Identify friction points in customer journeys  
- Spot themes across thousands of data points  

AI accelerates insights so PMs can focus on asking better questions.

---

# Phase 2 — Validate

Validation determines whether the identified problem is real, valuable, solvable, and aligned with the business outcome.

Key activities:
- Create hypotheses  
- Build lightweight prototypes  
- Test concepts with users  
- Identify the right approach before committing resources  

Validation prevents:
- Building the wrong thing  
- Solving a problem that doesn’t matter  
- Investing in ideas without evidence  

### Using AI in Validation
AI helps PMs:
- Synthesize user interview transcripts  
- Identify themes in feedback  
- Analyze survey and support data  
- Quickly build multiple prototype variations  
- Run simulated experiments for directional testing  

AI enables faster, cheaper, smarter validation cycles.

---

# Phase 3 — Build

The PM partners with Engineering and Design to build the product.

Key responsibilities:
- Translate validated insights into **user stories**  
- Create or refine the **Product Requirements Document (PRD)**  
- Define **acceptance criteria**  
- Align teams on scope and feasibility  
- Prevent scope creep unless justified  
- Ensure development aligns to the defined business outcomes  

### How PMs Write User Stories
As a <type of user>,
I want to <action>,
so that <benefit>.


### Using AI in the Build Phase
AI can:
- Convert feature descriptions into PRDs  
- Suggest edge cases for acceptance criteria  
- Draft user stories  
- Generate UX copy  
- Spot duplicate or outdated backlog items  

---

# Phase 4 — Launch

This is where users first interact with the new product or feature. Launch is not one event — it is a strategy.

Key activities:
- Coordinate with Marketing, Sales, and Customer Success  
- Decide on **free vs paid feature** placement  
- Plan rollout strategy (beta, limited release, geo-based release, etc.)  
- Enable PLG loops (in-app prompts, demos, referrals)  
- Ensure the success team has messaging, guides, and enablement assets  

Example:  
A company may release a feature first in **PA**, then **NY**, then **CA** to control risk.

### Using AI in Launch
AI helps:
- Create launch positioning  
- Draft marketing copy  
- Predict which segments will adopt faster  
- Build PLG nudges and personalized onboarding flows  

---

# Phase 5 — Evaluate

Go-live is not the end of product development.
After launch, PMs analyze:

- What are users doing?  
- Where are they getting stuck?  
- Is adoption happening as expected?  
- Are we achieving the business outcome defined in Phase 0?  

Key data sources:
- Usage analytics  
- Feature adoption reports  
- Support tickets  
- NPS and in-app surveys  
- Sales and CS feedback  

### Using AI in Evaluation
AI helps PMs:
- Analyze large usage datasets  
- Cluster user behaviors  
- Identify friction zones  
- Summarize qualitative feedback  
- Flag anomalies or churn indicators  

---

# Phase 6 — Iterate

Iteration closes the loop.

PMs now:
- Make improvements  
- Address feedback  
- Refine UX  
- Add missing functionality  
- Re-align the roadmap as necessary  

This is the stage where product-market fit is repeatedly strengthened.

### Using AI in Iteration
AI can:
- Suggest improvements based on usage and churn signals  
- Create variations of UX copy or flows  
- Identify patterns in evolving user behavior  
- Predict which changes will improve outcomes  

---

# Summary of the Product Management Lifecycle

The PMLC is not linear — it is cyclical.  
Each phase depends on the previous one, and all phases serve the **business outcome**, not the feature checklist.

AI enhances every stage:

- **Define** → predict impact  
- **Discover** → analyze qualitative and quantitative data  
- **Validate** → rapid prototyping & theme extraction  
- **Build** → PRDs, user stories, acceptance criteria  
- **Launch** → personalized GTM  
- **Evaluate** → deep insights at scale  
- **Iterate** → intelligent prioritization  

With AI, PMs deliver better products faster — and with greater confidence.
